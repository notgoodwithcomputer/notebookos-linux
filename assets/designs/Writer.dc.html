The Long Way Home

Notes written at a chrome desk, in the hour before the bakeries open.

There is an hour in this city — after the last express has rattled through and before the bakeries light their ovens — when the whole block seems to be holding its breath. I have learned to write inside it.

The desk faces the window on purpose: chrome legs, a slab of pale wood, a lamp the color of weak tea. Nothing on it earns its place unless I use it before noon.

What the morning is for

Mostly the morning is for getting the first sentence wrong, and then wrong again, until the wrongness wears a groove the right one can slide into. It is slow work and it does not photograph well.

A paragraph is just a room you build to stand in for a while. If the light is good, you stay.

By the time the trains fill again I have usually traded the essay I meant to write for the one that actually needed writing. This is not a loss. It is the long way home, which is the only way I seem to know.
